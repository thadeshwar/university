The daily timetable

Same shape every week, all year.

Mon – Fri
Lecture, 75–90 min. Watch the assigned lectures with full attention.
Recitation, 60–75 min. Exercises, code-along, or problem sets from that lecture. Never two lecture blocks back to back — watch, do, stop.
Saturday
Lab day, 3–4 hrs. Applied track only: projects, fine-tunes, builds.
Sunday
Review day, 1–2 hrs. Re-derive the week from memory, update the cheat sheet, take the weekly quiz with Claude, preview next week.
Daily
ENG 30 — the engineering half hour. Tooling and fluency: Python drills, shell & git, Docker, serving, ML system design. The specific assignment sits inside each week card below. First thing to pause if a week collapses.

Attendance policy: miss a day, don't double up — continue. Streaks beat catch-up. Two missed weeks means we re-plan, not cram.

Ten courses to rule them all

Ten whole video courses, in order, one at a time — each owns a domain, none overlaps Depth. Roughly 240 hours across the same fifteen months in dead time and spare evenings, never desk hours. Watch, don’t do; Depth owns the exercises.

Start

CMU 11-711 — Advanced NLP · Graham Neubig

Course site · ~25 hrs. Language models end to end, refreshed yearly — this year’s conversation topics, taught rigorously. The right opener.

DL

Michigan EECS 498 — Deep Learning for Computer Vision · Justin Johnson

Course site · ~22 hrs. Deep learning done properly, by CS231n’s co-creator — the fundamentals plus the entire vision story.

School 2

NYU — Deep Learning · Yann LeCun & Alfredo Canziani

Course site · ~30 hrs. The other school of thought — energy-based models, self-supervised learning — from the founder himself.

Generative

Stanford CS236 — Deep Generative Models · Stefano Ermon

Course site · ~25 hrs. VAEs, GANs, flows, diffusion — everything behind image, video, and audio generation.

Robotics

Princeton MAE 345 — Introduction to Robotics · Anirudha Majumdar

Lecture videos · ~25 hrs. Feedback control, motion planning, state estimation and mapping, vision and learning — the whole field in one true introduction, on real drones.

Hardware

ETH Zürich — Computer Architecture · Onur Mutlu

Lectures · watch the GPU, memory, and accelerator half, ~15 hrs. Why AI runs on GPUs and what a TPU actually is, from the most-watched architecture lecturer online.

Theory

Caltech CS156 — Learning from Data · Yaser Abu-Mostafa

18 lectures · ~18 hrs. Why any of it works: VC theory, generalization, overfitting. Placed here, after the landscape, so the question is interesting.

Probability

Harvard Stat 110 — Probability · Joe Blitzstein

Course site · ~40 hrs. The foundation under all of it, taught by its most beloved lecturer. Dip in by topic.

Applied

Udemy — AI Engineer Core Track: LLM Engineering, RAG, QLoRA, Agents · Ed Donner

In Udemy Business · 33.5 hrs, 210 lectures. Skim, don’t study: APIs, RAG, agents, fine-tuning — much of it you already live at work. Take the corners you haven’t touched, skip the rest.

Build

fast.ai — Practical Deep Learning for Coders · Jeremy Howard

course.fast.ai · ~15 hrs. The build-it-all close: top-down, working models every lesson — far more satisfying once you know why they work.

Standing rules

  1. Evals before models. Always.
  2. Code is typed, never pasted. The repo is the transcript.
  3. Math gets exercises, not notes. One cheat-sheet page per course, maximum.
  4. Sunday quiz with Claude, every week. Say "quiz me on this week."
  5. Stuck for 30 minutes? Office hours. That's the chat, any hour.
  6. Streak beats intensity. Three hours daily over ten on Saturday.
  7. Explain one concept out loud per week to another human. Khushi counts.
  8. The study MacBook runs only the university. No Slack. Ever.
  9. The wedding break is sacred. So is sleep.
  10. The 31 papers join on Sundays from Term 4 — by then they'll read like course notes.
  11. The calendar is frozen. Structure changes only at term finals, with the professor. Mid-term shiny things go to parking-lot.md, reviewed Sundays.
